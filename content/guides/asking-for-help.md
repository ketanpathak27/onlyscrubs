Asking for help is part of the job. Teams that treat it as a personal flaw create unsafe shifts. You can get better at the ask, and you can debrief afterwards without turning a real person into a story.

This is communication guidance. It is not counselling, not a critical-incident procedure, and not clinical advice. If there is immediate danger, call 000. If your workplace has an emergency number or a duress process, use that as well.

## Ask early, and ask a person

The useful time to ask is when you are uncertain, not after you have improvised. Find the person who is actually responsible: your buddy, the team leader, the midwife you are working with, your facilitator. A group chat is not that person.

Sentences that work:

- "I have not done this here. Can you watch me, or do it with me?"
- "I am not sure this is in my scope. Who should I check with?"
- "I need a second person before I continue."
- "I am worried, and I want someone to review this with me now."

You do not need a perfect ISBAR to start. If the situation is moving, use the shape in the [handover guide](/guides/isbar-handover.html): who you are, what you need, and how soon. Detail can follow.

If the first person brushes you off and you are still worried, ask the next person up. Worry that you cannot explain is still a reason to get someone else in the room. You can say, "I cannot name it, and I do not want to be alone with it."

## Students and new grads

You are supposed to need people. A placement or a grad year that praises silence is not a high standard. Agree with your buddy, on day one, how you should interrupt them. Some people want a hand on the shoulder. Some want you to wait at the desk. The agreement stops you hovering, and it stops you delaying.

If you are regularly left alone with work you have not been signed off to do, tell your university facilitator or graduate educator that day. Write down the general facts: the date, the kind of task, and who you told. Leave out names of people in your care.

Being snapped at when you ask is unpleasant. It is also data. If it happens once on a terrible shift, you can let it go. If it is the culture, your educator needs to know. You can describe behaviour without a public call-out.

## After the shift

Debrief with someone who is allowed to hear it. That might be your facilitator, a clinical educator, an employee assistance program if your employer provides one, or a colleague who was there and is still bound by the same confidentiality. It is not a story for a party, a dating app, or a public post.

A short debrief has three parts:

1. What happened, in terms that do not identify a patient, a family, or a workplace. "A sudden deterioration at the end of a late shift" is enough for a friend. The clinical detail belongs with the people who were involved and with the formal process.
2. What you did, and what you were unsure about.
3. What you need now: sleep, a roster change, a skill to practise in the lab, or a proper review.

If there is a formal debrief, a riskman-style report, or a speaking-up process, use the one your organisation names. This website is not that process. Do not put the report text into OnlyScrubs, including the confessions feed. Raising a safety concern, including when a mandatory notification might apply, is covered in [when and how to speak up](/guides/speaking-up-about-safety.html). That page is an overview, not legal advice.

Friends and family can support you without the details. "It was a heavy shift and I am sad" is a complete sentence. If they push for the story, you can say, "I cannot tell you that part, and I still want the company."

## When it follows you home

A hard shift can sit in your body for days. Sleep, food, and a conversation help many people. If you are not sleeping, if you are replaying it constantly, if you are drinking to switch it off, or if you feel hopeless, get a human whose job is health. Your GP is a starting point. Nurses, midwives and students can call Nurse & Midwife Support, 24 hours, on [1800 667 877](https://www.nmsupport.org.au/).

Other lines, if you need them:

- Lifeline, 13 11 14, [lifeline.org.au](https://www.lifeline.org.au/get-help/)
- Beyond Blue, 1300 22 4636, [beyondblue.org.au](https://www.beyondblue.org.au/get-support)
- 13YARN, 13 92 76, for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, [13yarn.org.au](https://www.13yarn.org.au/)
- Emergency, 000

The breathing exercise on the [wellbeing page](/wellbeing.html) is a few minutes of pacing your breath. It is not treatment, and it is not a debrief. Use it as a pause, then still talk to someone if the shift is stuck.

## What we will not publish

OnlyScrubs will not publish the story of what happened to a patient, a named colleague, or a named workplace. That includes "de-identified" versions. The [submission rules](/submission-rules.html) are the line. Asking for help belongs with people who can hold it safely. The internet is not one of those people. Once you are registered, the same habit is in the [first graduate year guide](/guides/first-graduate-year.html).
