Continuing professional development is the learning you do after you qualify so that your practice does not freeze on the day you graduated. The Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia sets the requirement in a registration standard. This page explains the standard that was in force when it was checked on 17 November 2026. It is not clinical advice, and it is not the standard. If the Board changes the rule, their page wins.

OnlyScrubs is not the NMBA and not Ahpra. We are not approving your CPD record.

## The hours in the current standard

The NMBA's [registration standard for continuing professional development](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-Standards/Continuing-professional-development.aspx) and the Board's own explainer, [understanding CPD](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-Standards/Understanding-CPD-for-nurses-and-midwives), set the minimum hours. Checked against those pages:

- A registered nurse or an enrolled nurse: at least 20 hours in the registration period.
- A midwife: at least 20 hours.
- If you are registered as both a nurse and a midwife, you meet the requirement for each profession. The Board's explainer adds those to 40 hours.
- A nurse practitioner, or a midwife with a scheduled medicines endorsement, has additional hours on top of the base 20, described in the standard as 10 further hours on the endorsed practice. Read the table rather than a summary in a slide.
- If you were registered for only part of the year, pro rata hours apply. The explainer sets them out in bands, from at least 5 hours when registration has been held for 0 to 3 months, up to the full 20 when it has been held for more than 9 months. Endorsements have their own smaller pro rata table.

In May 2026 the Board, with Ahpra, consulted on a revised CPD standard, including a proposal for interactive hours. A consultation is not the rule. Until a new standard is approved and given a start date, you declare against the standard that is in force. Check the NMBA news page before you renew, in case that date has arrived.

## What counts, in plain language

The standard wants learning that is relevant to your context of practice. A study day on the work you actually do is easier to justify than a random webinar you slept through. The hours are yours to plan. Your employer may offer education. That can count when it meets the standard. It does not replace the standard, and a shift where you were simply busy is not automatically CPD.

Keep evidence. The standard tells you what to keep and for how long. A sensible private note is the date, the hours, what you did, and why it mattered to your practice. Do not put patient names, bed numbers or workplace gossip in the note. "I attended a workshop on handover communication" is a learning record. A story about a particular person is not.

New graduates sometimes think the graduate program "is" the CPD. The program is a job with support. You still plan the hours the standard asks for. A study day inside the program may be part of the evidence. Write it down when it happens. Future-you, doing a renewal on a day off, will not remember the title.

## A simple way to start in the first year

You do not need a perfect portfolio in week one. You need a habit.

Pick a small number of gaps you already know you have. Not clinical instructions from a website. Things like "I want to be clearer in handover", "I want to understand the escalation path", or "I want to read the code of conduct properly". The [standards guide](/guides/nmba-standards-for-practice.html) is a reading list, not a course. The [ISBAR guide](/guides/isbar-handover.html) is practice at talking to colleagues.

Then notice what your employer and your university already offer: supernumerary education, a journal club, a skills workshop. Log the ones you actually attend. Add one thing you choose yourself, so the record is not only whatever was rostered.

If you are dual qualified, label the hours. A session that truly serves both nursing and midwifery can be discussed with the standard in front of you. Do not double-count a session into both columns because it would be convenient. The explainer is the referee.

## Renewal, and the declaration

When you renew, you declare that you meet the standard. The declaration is yours. Ahpra can ask to see the evidence. "I think I did about 20 hours" is a weak answer if they ask. A list with dates is a calm one.

If you had a long period of leave, or you registered late in the year, read the pro rata table before you panic and before you invent hours. The table is more generous to a short registration than rumour is. If you cannot meet the hours, contact Ahpra rather than guessing at the form. This site cannot lodge anything for you.

Registration itself, including the first application, is covered in [AHPRA registration as a new grad](/guides/ahpra-registration-new-grad.html). CPD is what happens after that permission exists.

## What not to buy in a hurry

You do not need an expensive folder with someone else's logo to meet the standard. You need relevant learning and a record you could show. Be wary of anyone selling "NMBA-approved CPD" as if the Board had endorsed their product. The Board publishes its own standard. A product can be useful and still not be endorsed. The templates on the OnlyScrubs [shop](/shop.html), including a CPD tracker in the nursing toolkit, are independent. They are not an NMBA product.

If the learning is stressing you more than the job, that is a separate problem. Nurse & Midwife Support is on [1800 667 877](https://www.nmsupport.org.au/). CPD is a professional obligation. It is not a punishment, and it is not a race.
