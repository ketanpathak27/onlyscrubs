Tax time produces a lot of confident nonsense in the tea room. Someone will tell you that every pair of shoes is a deduction, and someone else will tell you that nothing is. Neither of them is the Australian Taxation Office. This page is general information only. It is not tax advice, not a private ruling, and not a list of amounts you can claim. Checked against ATO material on 17 November 2026.

The occupation guide is the [nurses and midwives income and work-related deductions](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/income-deductions-offsets-and-records/guides-for-occupations-and-industries/l-q/nurses-and-midwives-income-and-work-related-deductions) page. Read that page for the current categories. If a sentence here is vaguer than the ATO, that is on purpose.

## The test the ATO actually uses

For a work expense, you generally need three things: you spent the money yourself and were not reimbursed, the expense is directly related to earning your income, and you have a record. If your total work-related claim is more than A$300, the ATO wants written evidence such as receipts. That A$300 line is in the ATO's self-education instructions for 2025–26, not a nursing special.

Self-education has a tighter test. The [myTax 2026 self-education instructions](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/your-tax-return/instructions-to-complete-your-tax-return/mytax-instructions/2026/deductions/work-related-expenses/work-related-self-education-expenses) say the course has to maintain or improve the specific skills of your current work, or be likely to increase your income from that work. You cannot claim study that only gets you a new job, or study you did when you were not employed. An entry-to-practice degree, while you are still a student and not working as a nurse or midwife, usually fails that test. A course you do after you are employed, because the job needs it, might pass. "Might" is the point. The ATO page decides, not this one.

You cannot claim repayments of HELP or HECS-HELP. The same instructions say so.

## The sorts of things the occupation guide is about

The nurses and midwives guide is where the ATO talks about the job specifically. People usually go there for compulsory uniforms and protective clothing, laundry of those items, union or professional association fees, the work-related portion of a phone, and tools you bought for the job. Home-to-work travel is the classic thing that is not deductible. Ordinary clothes that you could wear on the street are another. The guide's wording is the one to follow, including any dollar method it currently allows for laundry. Do not reuse a number from an old year.

Commonwealth Prac Payment, if you received it, is taxable income. The Department of Education says students have to handle that with the ATO. It is not a deduction. It is money in.

Keep the receipt, and a note of the work portion if you also use the thing at home. A spreadsheet on your own computer is fine. Do not put patient details in it. Do not post your tax return anywhere, including here.
