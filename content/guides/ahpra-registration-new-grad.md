Registration is the legal permission to practise as a nurse or a midwife in Australia. A graduate program, a contract, and a name badge do not replace it. This page walks through the public steps in plain language. It is not clinical advice, not a decision from the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia, and not a promise that an application will be granted.

The Board and the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency publish the process. If a sentence here and a sentence on their site disagree, use theirs. Pages move. Fees change. The links at the bottom are the source.

## Start from the official process, not a group chat

Ahpra's [registration process](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Registration/Registration-Process.aspx) is the map. The NMBA's [registration standards](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-Standards.aspx) are the rules the application is checked against. New graduates of an approved Australian program usually apply for general registration in the division that matches the qualification: registered nurse, enrolled nurse, or midwife. Some people apply in more than one division. The form asks which one. Do not guess from a friend's screenshot.

Create the account in your own name, with the identity documents the form lists. Do not ask someone else to "just finish it" on a shared login. You are declaring that the answers are true.

## What the application is usually asking for

The exact checklist is on the form. These are the pieces people trip over, described only so you know where to look.

**Identity.** You will be asked to prove who you are. Use the documents the form accepts. A photo that does not look like you, or a name that does not match the university record, slows things down. If your name has changed, gather the linking document before you start, not after the first email asking for it.

**Criminal history.** The [criminal history standard](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-Standards/Criminal-history.aspx) explains what must be disclosed. Ahpra arranges the check as part of the application. Disclose what the standard requires. A friend saying "that one does not count" is not the standard. If you are unsure whether something must be declared, read the standard and, if you still cannot tell, ask Ahpra. Do not put the story of the charge on a public website, including this one.

**English language.** The [English language skills standard](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-Standards/English-language-skills.aspx) applies to initial registration. Graduates of an NMBA-approved program in a listed country often meet it through the education pathway, but you have to read the current pathway and see whether your program and your schooling fit. If you need a test, book it early. A job offer does not waive the standard.

**Your qualification.** For many Australian graduates the education provider confirms completion. Ask your university what they send, and when. Applying before the result exists is normal for some cohorts and impossible for others. The form, not a rumour, says whether a pending completion is accepted. Keep the student number and the official course name handy. "I did nursing at uni" is not the name of the award.

**Professional indemnity insurance.** The [PII standard](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-Standards/Professional-indemnity-insurance.aspx) requires arrangements that cover your practice. Many employees are covered by their employer's insurance while they are working in that job. That is not a universal rule, and it does not cover practice outside the job. Read the standard, then read what your employer or union actually provides. This page cannot tell you that you are covered.

**The fee.** There is an application and registration fee. The current figure is on the NMBA [fees page](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-and-Endorsement/Fees.aspx). Do not copy a dollar amount from a chat. Pay the amount the form shows on the day you submit.

## While you wait

You cannot practise as a registered nurse or midwife until registration is granted, even if orientation is on the roster. Tell the employer the truth about where the application sits. "I have applied" and "I am registered" are different sentences.

Answer Ahpra's emails. The delays people describe are often a missing document, not a mystery. Check the junk folder. If they ask for something, send that thing, not a long explanation of your placement.

Keep a private note of the date you applied and the documents you uploaded. Do not include patient information in that note. There should be none in a registration file anyway.

## After registration is granted

Your name appears on the public register. Check the spelling and the division. Registration then has to be renewed, and you have to meet the standards that continue after the first day, including [continuing professional development](/guides/cpd-for-new-grads.html) and recency of practice. The CPD guide on this site points at the NMBA standard. It does not log your hours for you.

Registration does not pick your graduate job. State rounds are a separate process. See [graduate programs by state](/guides/graduate-programs-by-state.html). It also does not teach you the local workplace. The [first graduate year](/guides/first-graduate-year.html) page is about that transition, still in general terms.

If you are using social media while you wait for a number, read [social media and professionalism](/guides/social-media-and-professionalism.html) before you post a "finally registered" story that names a service or a patient.

## What to do if something looks wrong

If the online form will not accept a document, use the contact path on the Ahpra site. If you think a decision is wrong, the notification and review paths are also on that site, not in a comment under a confession. Email hello@onlyscrubs.org only if a sentence on this page is out of date. Do not send identity documents here.

You are allowed to find the process confusing. The remedy is the official page, read slowly, not a stranger's timeline.
