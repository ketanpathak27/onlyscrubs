Placement is when the timetable and the bank account have an argument. Rent does not pause because you are on a ward. This page is general information only, for students in Australia. It is not a decision about whether you will be paid, and it is not clinical advice. Figures below were checked on 17 November 2026. The official pages can move the numbers. The calculator uses the figures stored for this site, not a guess typed into the article.

## The costs people actually meet

Write your own list. Typical lines, none of which are a quote:

- travel, or a second rent if the placement is away from home
- parking, or a public transport pass
- food you would not have bought at home
- uniforms or shoes if you still need them
- childcare or lost shifts from the job that normally pays the rent
- vaccinations, checks, or a police check if your university has not already covered them

The [first clinical placement](/guides/first-clinical-placement.html) guide is about the shift itself. This one is about the money around it. [Budgeting](/guides/budgeting.html) is the broader student version.

## Commonwealth Prac Payment

From 2025, eligible nursing, midwifery, teaching and social work students can receive the Commonwealth Prac Payment while on a mandatory placement. The Department of Education's [FAQ](https://www.education.gov.au/commonwealth-prac-payment-cpp/frequently-asked-questions-cpp) says the 2025 weekly amount was A$331.65 and the 2026 amount is A$338.60. It is benchmarked to the single Austudy rate and indexed on 1 January. [Study Assist](https://www.studyassist.gov.au/financial-and-study-support/student-income-support) describes the same payment.

You do not get it for being on placement. The public criteria include being a domestic student in a Commonwealth supported place, in an eligible course, on a mandatory placement that averages 30 hours a week, or a midwifery continuity-of-care placement that stops you working. You also need to be on a listed income support payment, or meet a need-to-work test and an income test. The FAQ describes that test as working, on average, more than 15 hours a week over four weeks of normal study, and individual income that is not more than A$1,536.37 a week before tax in that period, including State, Territory or Commonwealth scholarships. Exceptional circumstances exist. Your university administers the claim. This website does not.

The payment is taxable. The Department of Education says you are responsible for the tax. It can also matter for Centrelink. Ask them, not a group chat.

Some states and universities advertise their own grants. Amounts and closing dates change. Use the current state health or university page. This guide does not copy those amounts, because a stale scholarship figure is worse than none.

## Using the sketch

Put in the number of weeks and what you think the placement will cost you on top of a normal study week. The page multiplies the weeks by A$338.60 and shows the gap. A gap does not mean you have been refused the payment. A surplus does not mean you are eligible. It is arithmetic, so you can see the size of the hole before the roster starts.
