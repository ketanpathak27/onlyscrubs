A rental bond is money held while you rent. At the end it comes back unless there is a lawful claim, such as unpaid rent or damage that is more than fair wear and tear. Who holds the money, how long you have to mark the entry report, and how a disagreement is decided are different in every state and territory. This page is general information for students and shift workers who rent. It is not legal advice. Laws and forms change. Use the official body for the place you actually rent, and a tenant advice service if you are in a dispute.

Shift work makes the paperwork easier to miss. You move in on a day off, the roster eats the next week, and the condition report is still in a bag. Put the return date in your phone the day you get the keys.

## The entry report is the document that matters

Dated photos help later. Photograph each room, including inside cupboards and the oven, and anything already marked or broken. Keep a copy of the report you returned, and the bond receipt or bond number. A private spreadsheet does not replace the official form.

These return times were read on the official pages on 17 November 2026:

- **Victoria.** Return the condition report within 5 business days of moving in. Weekends and public holidays do not count. If you were not given a report, you can complete your own and give it to the rental provider in that same time. Source: [Consumer Affairs Victoria, condition reports](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/condition-reports).
- **New South Wales.** Complete your part, take photos, and return one copy to the landlord or agent within seven days of moving in. Keep the other copy. Source: [NSW Government, rental property condition reports](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/rules/rental-property-condition-reports).
- **Queensland.** Return the signed entry condition report within 7 days after the later of the day you occupied the place or the day you were given the report. The property manager or owner must send a signed copy back within 14 days. If there is a later dispute, the report and any photos can be evidence at QCAT. Source: [Residential Tenancies Authority, entry condition report](https://www.rta.qld.gov.au/starting-a-tenancy/entry-condition-report).
- **Western Australia.** You should receive two copies of the property condition report within 7 days after you move in. If you disagree with it, mark that on the report and return it. The information statement for tenants is the place to start: [Consumer Protection WA](https://www.consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/property-condition-reports).

South Australia, Tasmania, the ACT and the Northern Territory use their own forms. Do not borrow Victoria's five business days or New South Wales' seven days and assume they apply. Start at the official page for that place, linked below.

Report repairs in writing during the tenancy and keep the replies. A text that only you can see is a weak record.

## Leaving

Official guidance generally expects the place to be reasonably clean, and in the condition it was in at the start, allowing for fair wear and tear, with keys and devices returned. Fair wear and tear is ordinary deterioration from reasonable use. Damage is not the same thing. If you and the owner cannot agree, the dispute body in that state decides. Take exit photos from the same angles as the entry photos, go to the final inspection if the roster allows, and keep a receipt or message that shows the keys went back.

In Victoria, if a rental provider wants to claim bond, Consumer Affairs Victoria says they must give you supporting evidence at least 3 business days before they make the claim. That evidence can be an invoice, a receipt, a quote or photographs, and it must not contradict the condition report. A bond dispute can go to VCAT. The full steps are on [CAV's bond claims and refunds page](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/bond/bond-claims-and-refunds). Bonds in Victoria are held through the [RTBA](https://rentalbonds.vic.gov.au/).

## Where the money is claimed

Use the official process. A snapshot from 17 November 2026:

- **New South Wales.** Claim through the process on [getting your bond back](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/renting-a-place-to-live/getting-your-bond-back). Read the response time on that page before you assume a refund date.
- **Queensland.** Request a refund through the [RTA](https://www.rta.qld.gov.au/refund). Where a bond was lodged on or after 30 September 2024, the RTA says a property manager or owner who claims or disputes a refund must give the tenant supporting evidence within 14 days. That fact sheet is [here](https://www.rta.qld.gov.au/forms-resources/factsheets/rental-bonds-for-rooming-accommodation-fact-sheet). Do not sign a blank refund form.
- **Western Australia.** A release can be started by someone named on the bond. The steps are on [releasing a bond](https://www.consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/releasing-bond). Answer any notice by the date it states.
- **South Australia.** Agreed refunds go through [Residential Bonds Online](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/housing/renting-and-letting/residential-bonds/bond-refunds).
- **Tasmania.** [MyBond](https://cbos.tas.gov.au/topics/housing/renting/bonds/claiming-a-bond) is the system. Consumer, Building and Occupational Services says an owner or agent must start a claim within three days of receiving the keys, and a tenant cannot start a claim until three days after the keys go back.
- **Australian Capital Territory.** Refunds are requested through the [ACT Revenue Office](https://www.revenue.act.gov.au/rental-bonds/how-to-request-a-bond-refund).
- **Northern Territory.** The landlord or agent holds the security deposit. Start at [when you are owed bond money](https://nt.gov.au/property/renters/moving-out/when-you-are-owed-bond-money) and follow that page's timing. Do not assume another state's deadline.

If you disagree with a claim, do not ignore the notice. The official dispute path is the one that counts. Early advice is available from [Tenants Victoria](https://tenantsvic.org.au/), the [Tenants' Union of NSW](https://www.tenants.org.au/), [Tenants Queensland](https://tenantsqld.org.au/), or the tenant service in your state.
