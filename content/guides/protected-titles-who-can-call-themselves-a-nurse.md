A lanyard is not a qualification. Neither is a LinkedIn headline written after nights, when the coffee is a rumour and the "final" roster has already been edited. In Australia, "nurse" is a legal title. Checked on 18 November 2026, the public pages say who may use it.

General information only, not clinical, legal or financial advice. It is not clinical advice. OnlyScrubs is not Ahpra, not the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia, and not a court.

## The Queensland case, without a name

On 17 November 2026 the NMBA reported that Ahpra had prosecuted a person under section 116(1) of the Health Practitioner Regulation National Law. The [news item](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/News/2026-11-17-Fake-nurse-gets-good-behaviour-bond) says she was an unregistered assistant in nursing, had started but not finished nursing qualifications, and had never been registered. She pleaded guilty in the Warwick Magistrates' Court on 12 November 2026 to one count of describing herself as a qualified and practising nurse. The court imposed a good behaviour bond of 2,500 dollars, recorded no conviction, and ordered 1,750 dollars in costs. The NMBA did not name her. This page will not name her, and it will not guess.

The role did not require registration. The offence was claiming to be a registered nurse, including in correspondence about care and in a teacher aide application. Ahpra's chief executive, Justin Untersteiner, said patients expect an honest account of qualifications. The NMBA chair, Dr Paula Medway, said it is a criminal offence to claim to be a registered nurse if you are not qualified and registered.

## What the law protects

Ahpra's page on [reporting a criminal offence](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Notifications/Reporting-a-criminal-offence.aspx), reviewed 24 April 2026, lists the nursing and midwifery protected titles as nurse, registered nurse, nurse practitioner, enrolled nurse, midwife and midwife practitioner. It points to sections 113 to 123 of the National Law. Section 113, in the [ACT text on AustLII](https://www.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/act/consol_act/hprnl428/s113.html), restricts using a protected title. The offence is knowingly or recklessly taking or using the title in a way that could reasonably be expected to induce a belief the person is registered, unless they are. Each state and territory applies the National Law, so the text in a case is the one in force there. Ahpra's page on [titles in advertising](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Resources/Advertising-hub/Resources-for-advertisers/Titles) says an unregistered person cannot use a protected title or hold themselves out as registered.

That same Ahpra page states maximum penalties of 60,000 dollars for an individual or 120,000 dollars for a corporation, and/or up to three years' imprisonment, per offence. A court can impose less. Warwick was a bond and no conviction. The maximum is not a forecast.

"Assistant in nursing" is not on the protected-title list. "Personal care assistant" is not on it either. They are job titles for unregistered work. Using them accurately is ordinary. Letting "nurse" stand alone, so a patient would think you are registered, is the problem sections 113 and 116 address.

Section 116 is the claims offence. Ahpra describes holding out as a title, word or description that could reasonably be understood to mean someone is a health practitioner, or is qualified to practise, when they are not. The Queensland charge was section 116(1). A CV, a job application, an email to another clinician, or a bio can be the claim. There is no night-shift exemption.

## Students, new graduates, and nurse practitioners

Students in an approved program, or on clinical training, must be registered as students. The NMBA [student registration fact sheet](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Registration-and-Endorsement/Student-Registration/Fact-sheet-FAQ-student-registration.aspx) says you do not apply yourself. The education provider sends the details to Ahpra. There is no certificate, and the student register is not public. Student registration is not general registration, and it does not authorise practice as a nurse or a midwife. The fact sheet does not give "student nurse" a special blessing. The safe public words are nursing student, midwifery student, or assistant in nursing. [Your first clinical placement](/guides/first-clinical-placement.html) is about being there as a student.

The NMBA [graduate fact sheet](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Codes-Guidelines-Statements/FAQ/Graduate-Applications-FAQs-NMBA) says graduates of approved programs need to be registered before they can start working as nurses and midwives. A contract and a graduate offer do not fill the gap. The steps are in [AHPRA registration as a new grad](/guides/ahpra-registration-new-grad.html). The job round is separate: the [graduate program hub](/guides/graduate-program-hub.html). Until the public register shows the right division, you do not practise under that title, and you do not use it.

Nurse practitioner is its own protected title. It needs an endorsement. The NMBA [endorsement standard](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/registration-standards/endorsement-as-a-nurse-practitioner.aspx) says the revised standard takes effect on 14 January 2027. The [October 2026 news](https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/News/2026-10-13-Advance-copy-NP-registration-standard-and-guidelines) is the advance copy. Until the endorsement is on the register, "NP" is not a nickname.

## The register, the bio, and a concern

The [public register](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Registration/Registers-of-Practitioners.aspx) is the check. Read the division, any endorsement, and any published condition. A student not appearing as a registered nurse is the expected result. If a badge or a bio says "registered nurse" and the register does not, believe the register.

Use, today, the title the register would support. Not the one you are studying toward, and not a title from another country unless this register shows it. [Social media and professionalism](/guides/social-media-and-professionalism.html) is the same rule for a caption. If you advertise a regulated health service, Ahpra's advertising rules apply, including the rule against testimonials. Ahpra's titles page also covers using a protected title about someone else. A roster that prints "RN" beside a name the register does not support is a question for the person in charge and, if it is holding out, for Ahpra. It is not a post.

A notification is for the health, conduct or performance of someone who is registered. Start at [Notifications](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Notifications.aspx). An unregistered person using a protected title is a criminal-offence report. Ahpra's reporting page asks for the criminal offences form, sent with any evidence to offence.complaints@ahpra.gov.au. If the matter is violence, sexual assault or fraud, that page says to tell the police as well. Do not investigate the person yourself, and do not publish their name. The NMBA's November item asked employers and other health professionals to contact Ahpra. It did not ask a comments thread to finish the case. [Speaking up about safety](/guides/speaking-up-about-safety.html) is the overview, and it is not legal advice.

If raising it sits heavily, Nurse & Midwife Support is [1800 667 877](https://www.nmsupport.org.au/). Lifeline is [13 11 14](https://www.lifeline.org.au/).

The printer will still jam. The title on the badge should still be the one the register can stand behind.
