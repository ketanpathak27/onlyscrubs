Most pre-registration nursing and midwifery placements in Australia are unpaid. The degree can still be full-time while the income stops, and the costs go up: travel, parking, shoes, uniforms, and sometimes a second rent closer to the placement. This page names those costs in general terms. It is not personal financial advice, tax advice, or a list of dollar amounts, because payments and grants change.

## Know which costs are coming

Make a list for the next placement block, on paper or in a notes app:

- Transport, including the early shift that starts before your usual bus.
- Parking, if driving is the realistic option. Weekly parking near large hospitals adds up faster than people expect.
- Uniform pieces and shoes that meet the rules. Buy the required colour first. Extras can wait.
- A placement that is away from home: accommodation, extra groceries, and the casual shifts you will not be able to work.
- Vaccination or compliance checks if the university does not cover them. Ask the faculty before you pay, in case there is a process.

Put a number next to each line from your own quotes, not from a stranger's post. Then you can see the gap between that total and the money you will actually have that fortnight.

## Income that might exist, and where to check it

Eligible students sometimes receive Youth Allowance or Austudy through Services Australia. Whether you qualify depends on your age, study load, and, for some payments, your parents' income or your partner's. The rules change. Read the current pages on the [Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/) website, or talk to their staff, rather than relying on a figure in a forum.

Tuition for Commonwealth supported places can often be deferred through the HELP scheme for eligible students. HELP is about course fees. It does not pay your rent or your parking. Study Assist, the Australian Government site for student loans, is the place to confirm what applies to your enrolment.

Universities often have one-off hardship grants, food pantries, or placement bursaries. Names differ. The student portal and the student union are the practical starting points. Some state health departments and nursing organisations advertise placement scholarships in some years and not in others. Search the current year. Do not budget as if last year's grant still exists until you have seen it in writing.

Casual work is real income and also real fatigue. A close-open around placement is how people arrive unsafe. If the only way to pay rent is a shift pattern that scares you, talk to the university financial counselling or hardship team before you assume there is no other option.

## Spending that is worth it, and spending that can wait

Shoes that meet the rule and still feel possible at hour eight are worth saving for. A second pair can wait until the first pair is proven. Scrubs in the wrong colour are not a bargain. Check the uniform guide before you buy a set because it was cheap online.

The [Scrub Rack](/scrub-rack.html) is for clean, ordinary scrub gear between people. It will not replace a mandated uniform with a logo, but it can help with a spare plain set if your rules allow it.

Textbooks: check the library's electronic copies and older editions your lecturer has said are acceptable before you buy new. Stationery does not improve grades. The [study guide](/guides/study-tips.html) is blunt about that.

Stethoscopes and other kit: buy what the unit outline requires, at the time it requires it. See the [gear guide](/gear-guide.html). A more expensive model is not a financial plan.

## A simple system

One account, or one labelled pocket in your banking app, for placement costs. Move a small amount into it on weeks you do have income. When the roster drops, you are not starting from zero.

Write the placement weeks on the same calendar as your bills. Direct debits do not care that you are on nights.

If you share bills, tell your household early that a placement fortnight will be thin. A clear sentence now is better than a quiet panic later.

## If you are already behind

Talk to the university financial counselling service, and to anyone you owe money, before fees and fines stack. Many providers have hardship processes. Avoid short-term loans with high fees as a way to get through a four-week placement. They often cost more than the parking they were meant to cover.

You can be a careful student and still be broke. The placement model is expensive. Using a grant, a payment you are eligible for, or a donated pair of scrubs is practical. It is not a failure to budget.

If money stress is tangled up with not coping, Nurse & Midwife Support ([1800 667 877](https://www.nmsupport.org.au/)) can help you think about the next conversation, and your GP or the university counselling service can help with the health side.
