A graduate or transition program is a job with extra support in the first year. It is not a national course, and it is not required by the NMBA before you can be registered. Public employers in each state and territory run their own rounds. Private hospitals, aged care and primary care often recruit on a different calendar, sometimes with no central match at all. This page lists the official public starting points and the habits that keep an application honest. It is not employment advice, not clinical advice, and not a ranking of services.

Dates move every year. Do not copy last year's opening day from a screenshot. Open the page and read the current round.

## Before the state page

You will be asked about registration. Some rounds want you registered before you start work. Some accept an application in progress. None of them can register you. That process is [AHPRA registration as a new grad](/guides/ahpra-registration-new-grad.html). You will also be asked for referees who have seen you in practice. Ask them before you type their names. Give them the dates.

Write a preference list you could live with, including a second and third choice. A program that exists only in your head is not a plan. Read what "supernumerary" and "rotation" mean in that employer's handbook, because the words are not identical everywhere. The [first graduate year](/guides/first-graduate-year.html) page is about the months after you are offered a place.

## New South Wales

NSW Health coordinates GradStart through the Nursing and Midwifery Office. The [GradStart handbook](https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/nursing/employment/Pages/gradstart-recruitment-handbook.aspx) and the [eligibility page](https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/nursing/employment/gradstart/Pages/eligibility.aspx) are the documents to read. Eligibility is tied to when you finish your degree and to whether you can be registered and work. The handbook, not a forum, has the dates for the round it covers. If you finished earlier or later than the window, read the eligibility rules before you spend a weekend on the form.

## Victoria

The Department of Health describes graduate programs on its [nursing and midwifery graduates](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/nursing-and-midwifery/nursing-and-midwifery-graduates) page. Public health services, and some private employers, use the graduate nursing and midwifery program match, often called the GNMP Match, which is run with the Postgraduate Medical Council of Victoria. Other employers advertise directly. A match is a preference system. It is not a guarantee, and a service that does not join the match will not appear in it. Incentives are sometimes advertised for particular cohorts. If a dollar figure matters to you, read it on the current Department of Health page. Do not treat an old news story as an offer.

## Queensland

Queensland Health runs a statewide [registered nurse and midwife graduate program](https://www.careers.health.qld.gov.au/nursing-and-midwifery-careers/nurse-and-midwife-graduate-program). Hospital and health services are part of one public system, and the careers page lists how to apply and who to contact in each service. Rural and remote options are described there too. Read the current round's dates on that site. A closed round does not mean the next one will open on the same day.

## Western Australia

WA Health uses GradConnect, coordinated by the Chief Nursing and Midwifery Office. The public page is [GradConnect](https://www.health.wa.gov.au/GradConnect). It covers participating public services and, in some rounds, private providers. Positions can be metropolitan, regional, rural or remote. The page says when the next information will be published. If the round you wanted has closed, use the WA Health job search the page points to rather than refreshing a dead link.

## South Australia

SA Health runs a Transition to Professional Practice Program for newly qualified registered nurses and midwives. The [TPPP page](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/careers/recruiting+now/transition+to+professional+practice+program/transition+to+professional+practice+program) is the place for the current programs, including midwifery and nursing streams. "Transition to professional practice" is the local name. It is the same kind of supported first year, not a different profession.

## Tasmania

The Tasmanian Department of Health recruits graduates into Transition to Practice roles and publishes the pathway from [nursing and midwifery careers](https://www.health.tas.gov.au/careers/career-options/nursing-and-midwifery-careers) and [graduate opportunities](https://www.health.tas.gov.au/careers/graduate-opportunities). Positions are also advertised through Tasmanian Government jobs. Because individual advertisements open and close, start at the Department's careers pages and follow the current application guide. Do not rely on a single job ad from a previous year.

## Australian Capital Territory

Canberra Health Services publishes its [graduate nursing and midwifery programs](https://www.canberrahealthservices.act.gov.au/careers/nursing-and-midwifery-careers/graduate-nursing-and-midwifery-programs). The public information describes streams across the Canberra Hospital campus and North Canberra Hospital, support from clinical development nurses or midwives, and the requirement to hold Ahpra registration before the program starts. Intakes fill. If the page says a round has closed, that is the fact for that round.

## Northern Territory

NT Health describes the Graduate Nurse and Midwife Program on its [graduate programs](https://health.nt.gov.au/careers/nursing-and-midwifery/career-structure-and-pathways/graduate-programs) page. Places are named by region, including the Top End, Central Australia, East Arnhem, Big Rivers and Barkly. Read the eligibility dates on that page. They are written as a window of graduation, and the window changes. The page also describes orientation and educator support in general terms. It is not a promise about a particular roster.

## If you miss the round

Missing a public round is common. It is not a verdict on whether you can practise. Private employers, rural services and later intakes exist. Registration can still proceed. See the [registration guide](/guides/ahpra-registration-new-grad.html) and keep a calm record of what you applied for, with no patient stories attached. Pay, penalties and agreements are a separate question, covered in [pay and penalty rates](/guides/pay-and-penalty-rates.html) only as a map to the official instruments.

No service on this list is recommended over another. OnlyScrubs is not those employers, and a link is not an advertisement of a regulated health service.
