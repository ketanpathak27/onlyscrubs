Pay questions travel faster than pay answers. Someone in a group chat will quote a Saturday rate from a different state, a different year, and a different kind of employer, and it will sound confident. This page will not do that. It is not industrial advice, not financial advice, and not clinical advice. It is a map to the documents that actually set rates.

If a number matters, read it in the instrument that covers your job, on the day you are looking. Do not copy a figure from here. There are no figures on this page, on purpose.

## Two different worlds: the award, and the agreement

A lot of private-sector and some aged-care nursing work is covered by the Nurses Award, a modern award of the Fair Work Commission. Its code is MA000034. The Fair Work Ombudsman keeps a plain-language [summary of the Nurses Award](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/employment-conditions/awards/awards-summary/ma000034-summary). The full award, including the penalty and overtime clauses, sits in the Fair Work Commission's award library. Search MA000034 there rather than trusting a screenshot.

Public hospitals usually do not pay you under that award. They pay you under an enterprise agreement for that state or territory health service. The agreement has its own classifications, its own penalties, and its own dates. A Victorian public rate and a Queensland public rate are not interchangeable, and neither is "the award rate" unless the agreement says it borrows one.

The [Australian Nursing and Midwifery Federation](https://www.anmf.org.au/) and its state branches publish information about the public-sector agreements they bargain. Your employer should also be able to point you at the agreement that covers you. Ask for the title and the year, then read that document. "We're on the EBA" is not a title.

## What people mean by penalty rates

Penalty rates are extra pay for times that are unsocial: evenings, nights, Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays, and often overtime. They are usually written as a percentage of an ordinary rate, or as a loaded rate, inside the award or the agreement. The percentages are not the same in every instrument, and they change when the instrument is varied.

The Fair Work Ombudsman explains the general idea of [penalty rates and allowances](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/penalty-rates-and-allowances). That page is about the system. It is not your payslip. Allowances are a cousin of penalties: things like a uniform allowance, a qualification allowance, or a remote-area allowance, if your instrument has them. Again, the list is in the document, not in a reel.

Overtime is not "I stayed twenty minutes to finish notes" unless the instrument and the local process say those minutes count. Some workplaces expect you to claim it. Some will not pay it unless it was authorised. Learn the local rule from your educator or payroll in your first fortnight. The habit of asking early is in [surviving your first rotation](/guides/first-rotation.html).

## How to check a payslip without starting a rumour

Look at four things.

1. The classification. New graduate registered nurses are not all on the same grade in every agreement. Enrolled nurses are on a different scale. Midwives may be on the nursing scale or a midwifery scale depending on the instrument. The name on the payslip should match the name in the agreement.
2. The ordinary hours. A fortnightly pay does not mean you worked a fortnight of identical shifts.
3. The penalties. Nights, weekends and public holidays should show as their own lines if they were worked and if the instrument pays them that way. A missing line is a question for payroll, not a confession.
4. The tax and super. Those are not set by the nursing award. The Australian Taxation Office publishes the current tax tables. Superannuation rules are on the ATO and the Fair Work site. This page will not estimate your take-home pay.

If a line looks wrong, ask payroll in writing and keep the reply. Take a payslip to your union if you are a member and you cannot get a clear answer. Do not post the payslip. It has your name, your employer and often a workplace. That is identifying, and it does not belong on OnlyScrubs.

## Graduate programs and the first payslip

A graduate program is still a job. The pay is the classification in the agreement or award, not a student stipend, unless a particular program says otherwise in its own documents. Some jurisdictions have advertised sign-on support for particular cohorts. If you see a dollar amount in a news story, check whether it still applies to your start date on the department's own page. The [graduate programs by state](/guides/graduate-programs-by-state.html) guide links those pages and does not repeat the amounts.

Registration is separate from pay. You can be offered a job and still be waiting on Ahpra. You should not be practising as a registered nurse or midwife until registration is granted. The steps are in [AHPRA registration as a new grad](/guides/ahpra-registration-new-grad.html).

## What this page will not do

It will not tell you whether you are underpaid. It will not compare states. It will not tell you to leave a job. Those are decisions for you, with the instrument in front of you and, if you want, a union or a lawyer. OnlyScrubs is not a union, not an employer, and not the Fair Work Ombudsman.

The joke, if you need one, is that the roster and the payslip are written by different people who have never met. The remedy is still the document, not a louder group chat.
