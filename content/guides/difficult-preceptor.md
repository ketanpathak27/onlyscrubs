A preceptor, buddy or facilitator is there to supervise your learning. Some are generous teachers. Some are busy, blunt, or a poor fit. A few cross a line into bullying or unsafe pressure. You can respond to each of those without diagnosing the person and without posting their name. This is general information. It is not clinical advice, not a workplace investigation, and not counselling.

If you are in immediate danger, call 000. If the problem is a safety concern about care, read [when and how to speak up](/guides/speaking-up-about-safety.html) as well. That page is an overview of reporting and mandatory notifications, not legal advice.

## Separate "hard feedback" from "this is not okay"

Hard feedback is specific, about the work, and possible to act on. "Your handover left out the plan. Next time start with what you need from me." That can sting and still be fair. You do not have to enjoy it. You can ask for one example and one thing to repeat tomorrow.

It is a different problem when the comments are about your body, your accent, your gender, your culture or your right to be there. It is a different problem when you are mocked for asking a question, left without supervision for work you were told not to do alone, or told to ignore a policy. Those are not "just a tough unit." Write down what was said, the date, and who else was there, in a private note. Leave out patient details. You are recording the behaviour toward you, not a clinical story.

Do not argue the whole relationship in the middle of a busy shift if you can help it. A short sentence is enough in the moment: "I want to do this safely. I need you to show me once." Then take the longer conversation to the person whose job is your program.

## Who to tell, in order

Students usually start with the university facilitator or the subject coordinator. The university has the agreement with the placement site. New graduates usually start with the educator or the graduate program coordinator, not with a public review. If you are employed, your contract and your state may also give you a path through a manager or a union. This page cannot choose the path. Ask: "Who is the right person for a concern about supervision?"

Use the [asking for help](/guides/asking-for-help.html) shape. What happened, in non-identifying words. What you need: a different buddy, a mediated conversation, or a move. What you have already tried.

Take a support person to a formal meeting if you are allowed to. Read any email before you send it. Angry late-night drafts feel accurate and read as unclear. Stick to dates and sentences you could say again in a calm room.

## What not to do with the story

Do not post it. Not in a student group, not as a confession, not as a "name and shame." Even without the name, a small cohort can identify a person, and you can harm your own process. The [social media guide](/guides/social-media-and-professionalism.html) and the [submission rules](/submission-rules.html) both say this. A vent to one trusted friend, with no clinical detail and no name, is different from a forum.

Do not try to win by matching the tone. A sharp reply in front of a patient, or in front of a group, becomes the story that gets remembered. You can be firm and still be brief.

Do not stop asking questions to prove you can cope. Unasked questions are how new people get hurt. The standards expect you to work within your capability. See the [standards guide](/guides/nmba-standards-for-practice.html).

## If it is affecting your health

Dreading every shift, not sleeping, or feeling ashamed to walk in are reasons to get support, not evidence that you are unsuited to the work. Nurse & Midwife Support is on [1800 667 877](https://www.nmsupport.org.au/), 24 hours, for nurses, midwives and students. Your GP is the right person for your health. Beyond Blue and Lifeline are on the [wellbeing page](/wellbeing.html).

You can also ask the program what a change of preceptor looks like. People move. Asking is not a failure. Staying silent until you disappear makes the next placement harder to explain.

## After it improves, or after you leave

Keep the private note until the placement or the rotation is finished and any process is closed. Then you can let it go. Write one line about what you learned to ask for next time. That line is yours. It does not need an audience.

A difficult preceptor is a problem to be handled in the right room. It is not content. The internet is the wrong room.
